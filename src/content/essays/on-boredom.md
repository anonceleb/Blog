Boredom is not the enemy. It is information.

When you feel bored, your mind is telling you that the current activity (or lack of activity) is not providing enough novelty, challenge, or meaning. Most people treat this signal as a bug to be patched with a phone, a video, or another tab.

## The modern avoidance

We have become extraordinarily good at never being bored:

- Infinite scroll
- Autoplay
- Notifications that arrive at perfectly random intervals

Each one is a tiny hit of stimulation designed to keep the mind slightly above the boredom threshold.

The cost is rarely discussed. Deep work, original ideas, and even basic self-knowledge all seem to require long stretches of *uncomfortable* mental quiet. Boredom is the price of entry.

## What boredom might actually be for

In a world of constant input, boredom may be one of the only remaining ways your brain forces you to:

1. Generate your own thoughts instead of consuming other people's
2. Notice what actually feels worth doing once the easy stimulation is removed
3. Sit with questions long enough for better questions to appear

The people who produce interesting work are often the ones who have made peace with long, boring periods. They don't fill every gap.

## A small practice

Try this: the next time you reach for your phone out of habit, wait thirty seconds. Notice what the boredom feels like in your body. Then decide whether you actually want stimulation, or whether you were just running from the silence.

Most of the time, the silence has something to say if you let it finish a sentence.
