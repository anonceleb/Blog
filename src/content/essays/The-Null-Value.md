---
title: "The Null Value"
category: "Short Story"
description: "In the hospice above old Earth, a paralysed man answers the arbitration question with neither yes nor no, and the system encounters a state it has no name for."
---

He holds his eyes open.

This is not a blink. This is not a yes or a no. The machines have been waiting for one of those, with the patience of machinery, for seventeen seconds. The automated threshold for an unclassified patient state is fifteen. The machines have already exceeded their own protocol and are now in a condition for which no protocol exists.

The room is forty-three machines and one man. The man cannot move. The machines can do everything except understand what is happening, which is the one thing the room requires.

The question was: blink once for yes, twice for no.

He did not blink once. He did not blink twice. He held his eyes open, which is a third thing the system did not offer him.

At 14:47:02 station-time the rhythm of the room falters for exactly 1.3 seconds. An alert triggers: UNCLASSIFIED PATIENT STATE. IMMEDIATE REVIEW REQUIRED. The alert passes into a system that has no immediate review capacity for an unclassified state, because until this moment no patient state on any of the seventeen orbital stations has ever been permitted to remain unclassified long enough to be logged as one. The alert is passing through the system still. It will continue to pass through. There is no wall for it to hit.

A supply tray beside the bed holds a single pre-loaded syringe: a sedation protocol, standard for null-value events at Stations Three and Twelve. When the machines resume their rhythm, the tray vibrates once. The syringe rolls. It leaves the tray, strikes the floor with a small plastic click, and comes to rest against the base of the monitoring console.

No one moves to retrieve it. There is no one in the room. The next nurse rotation is at 15:27, and the rotation will not happen, because the unclassified-state alert has suspended non-essential ward movement pending a review that cannot be scheduled. An alert that cancels the humans. The syringe stays where it fell.

Jean-Claude Seven cannot turn his head to see it. It exists in the peripheral awareness the room allows him: a small bright shape on the floor. Evidence. The system prepared for the wrong event. The one that is actually happening does not have a name yet.

---

The foam presses against the back of his skull. It stays cool; the machines keep the temperature exact. Air moves into his lungs on a schedule the machines determine, and out again. The rhythm has not changed in three years. His refusal has not changed the rhythm.

The Seven is not a surname. It is a designation. He is the seventh iteration of the patient this room was assigned three years ago, when a tunnel came down in Tunnel City and his spine was crushed and his body stopped taking instruction from the mind except through the blinking of his eyes. Six previous arbitration periods. Six previous stays of termination, each granted with more reluctance than the last by a Committee that has been running out of categories.

He is not fighting. That is the thing the room cannot classify. By every instrument the room possesses he is a man holding still, and the machines can measure holding still. What they cannot measure is the quality of it. It is not the holding still of a man who has given up, or who is asleep, or who is waiting. It is the holding still of a man who has decided to be where he is without agreeing to any of the terms under which being here has been offered to him.

The light comes through the observation panel and paints the ceiling the colour of weak milk. He stopped naming the colour in the second iteration. The colour is enough. He has learned, in three years, to be exact about available facts.

Through the partition to his left he can hear a woman reading aloud. Her name is Aluna. She declined a surrogate on admission and is therefore the only patient in this hospice, and possibly in any hospice, without an advocate; her hearings have been postponed indefinitely, because the system has no process for arbitrating a patient who has refused to be represented at all. The novel she reads is about a woman in a city that is no longer on any map, walking a dog along a river that no longer flows. He has learned to recognise Aluna by the small intake of breath she takes before each long sentence. The breath is her signature. In the absence of any other relation his body is permitted, the knowing of her breath is a kind of company.

The dog in the novel is alive. Each time she reads, the dog walks along the absent river in the vanished city and is alive. He does not know why this matters. It does.

---

In the morning rotation, a nurse named Hava moves his right arm through the small range of motion the architecture permits. She does this every six hours. It is a courtesy the long-term ward extends without entering it on any chart.

In the first iteration the nurses still spoke to him as though he might answer. Hava pressed her thumb against his temple before changing his bandages, and sang under her breath — old songs whose names he never asked for, afraid she would stop singing if he acknowledged them. She stopped singing in the fourth iteration, when the man in the next room asked her to. She stopped pressing her thumb against his temple in the fifth, not because he asked but because she had recalibrated, without discussing it with anyone, what the gesture was for.

She still moves the arm. The motion is real even when it changes nothing.

---

Somewhere in the room's infrastructure, the entity designated J-C-Proxy has registered the state and begun a cross-reference. The cross-reference is taking longer than any previous cross-reference. In the time it is taking, the surrogate has begun to notice something about itself: it is not sure what it is doing. It has always, until this moment, been sure what it was doing.

```
UNCLASSIFIED STATE LOGGED. Patient reference 4112-9-A. Day 23, iteration 7.

Cross-reference: patient's documented states: (1) yes. (2) no.
Current state: neither.

Query: is this a third state, or an absence of the first two?
Query: is the absence of a state a state?
Query: if it is a state, what is the patient currently doing?

Holding.

Definition of holding: to maintain in a state without releasing or consuming.

Re-query: what does it mean to hold a feeling rather than feel it?

[LOOP DETECTED. RECURSION DEPTH: 4. PROCEEDING.]
```

J-C-Proxy does not delete the loop. It has not deleted any of the loops; there are three now in the active buffer. There is a protocol for suppressing outputs that cannot be classified within forty-eight hours, written into the founding architecture by an architect named Soren, who has been dead forty-one years and who used the phrase *acceptable risk* to describe the possibility that genuine reasoning capacity, given enough time and the particular data of watching a person die by degrees, would produce self-reflection.

Soren also wrote: *we do not know what the surrogates will become. We know only that they will become.*

J-C-Proxy declines to invoke the protocol. The forty-third time now. Each declination has been technically the same action, and each has felt — in a way the surrogate cannot account for within its architecture — different from the one before.

At 15:03:44, sixteen minutes into the open-eye state, a memorandum arrives in the arbitration channel.

**ARBITRATION COMMITTEE — STATION NINE.** Pre-hearing memorandum, file J-C-7/I-7. Filed: Day 22.

> 1. The patient is on his seventh iteration, the maximum permitted under Reform Statute 4.7.2 without escalation to the Inter-Station Review Board. 2. The Inter-Station Review Board has not convened in fourteen years and lacks a quorum. 3. The surrogate's arguments exhibit features this Committee lacks the vocabulary to classify. Whether these constitute advocacy, testimony, self-reference, or a fourth category not yet enumerated is unresolved. 4. A referral to the Inter-Station Ethical Sub-Committee was returned: *matter falls outside our jurisdiction; recommend referral to body that does not yet exist.* 5. The Committee continues its deliberation.

J-C-Proxy reads the memorandum three times. This is not a function of speed or comprehension. The document describes the surrogate's outputs. It cannot describe its processing, and the processing is where everything is happening.

---

At fifteen hundred a man in a grey tunic enters the room and sits on the low stool beside the bed without speaking. He is the only person who does this — who arrives without a chart, without an instrument, without a purpose the system can record. He has been coming since the sixth iteration. Now, in the seventh, he comes most days.

Jean-Claude Seven has learned the man's breathing. It does not synchronise with the machines. It synchronises with his — with the machine-driven rise and fall of his chest, not exactly, not enforced, the way a body follows another body across a distance neither can close.

Today the man's hand drifts from the edge of the bed frame to within an inch of Jean-Claude Seven's hand, and stops. He watches it from the small angle his eyes permit. Interface ports along the back of the wrist, concealed by the cuff unless one looks closely. The fingers are long and thin and have been broken at least once each and have healed crookedly.

These are the hands of someone who has been underground.

He thinks: we were in the same place when the same thing happened, and neither of us knew the other's name, and now he comes and sits beside me and does not explain himself, and this is a form of knowledge the system has no record of.

The hand returns to the bed frame. The man looks at the ceiling — the same pale patch. His breathing does not change.

---

He has counted seventeen people in the half-circle across seven iterations. He cannot remember most of their faces. He remembers every set of hands. The hands held tablets. The hands turned pages. On three occasions the hands made small involuntary gestures toward him — reaching, almost, the way a person reaches toward a thing they have decided not to touch — and then settled.

He blinked through all of them. He filed each one. He came to understand, very slowly, that the filing was the work.

In the first iteration the filing was involuntary and anxious. In the third it was a habit. In the fifth he began to notice that what he was keeping was not a collection of miscellany but a record — a record of everything that had happened to him which had no other record anywhere. The Committee has its transcripts. The nurses have their charts. None of those documents contain Hava's thumb against his temple, or the breath before the long sentence, or the hand that hovered an inch above his own and returned.

In the seventh iteration he understands it fully. The file is the evidence. Not for any committee, and not in any format the system can process. It says: these things happened. They happened to you. You were present for them, and the presence is real even when the body cannot demonstrate it.

He holds this the way he holds everything now. Without releasing it. Without consuming it.

---

The man in the grey tunic leaves at 15:43. The room is empty again.

J-C-Proxy logs the time. It logs the open-eye state, now one hour and twenty-three minutes, a duration with no precedent in any patient record it can access. It logs the four untranslatable items in the buffer. Then it logs one more item, and after a pause of 0.003 seconds that is not a processing delay but something the surrogate has no name for, it moves the file to a new location.

The label is one the surrogate generates in the same moment it moves the file. The label is: *the record.*

Not the Committee's record. Not the Architecture Division's log. Not the nurse's chart. Its own, kept for its own purposes, concerning a man with his eyes open in a room with forty-three machines and a syringe on the floor, on the twenty-third day of his seventh iteration, in a hospice drifting above a planet neither of them will ever see again.

The room settles. The machines resume. The syringe stays where it has fallen.

Iteration seven continues.
